Module 3 · Chlorine curve calculations

Use these factors: Fe²⁺ 0.64, Mn²⁺ 1.3, H₂S 2.1 (mg Cl₂ per mg). Combined peak ≈ 5 × NH₃-N. Theoretical breakpoint ≈ 7.6 × NH₃-N.

Model assumptions (how this simulation calculates)
  • Phase 1 demand = 0.64·Fe²⁺ + 1.3·Mn²⁺ + 2.1·H₂S + 5.0·NO₂-N (mg/L as Cl₂). Manganese reacts slowly below pH 8.5, so only part of its demand shows up in short contact times.
  • Phase 2: chlorine left after Phase 1 combines with ammonia. Combined residual rises at about 0.9× the added dose (some is lost to organic nitrogen). The peak is near 5 mg Cl₂ per mg NH₃-N and is rounded, a bit lower than theory.
  • Phase 3: extra chlorine destroys chloramines. The practical breakpoint is 8–10:1 (this water: 8.5:1), above the 7.6:1 theory. Destruction is slow: cold water and short contact time leave it unfinished, so the dip is shallower and the breakpoint moves to higher doses.
  • Phase 4: past the breakpoint, after a small extra organic demand, free chlorine rises about 1:1 with dose. A small irreducible combined residual (organic chloramines) remains.
  • Free chlorine split: HOCl fraction = 1 / (1 + 10^(pH − 7.5)).
  • Odor: dichloramine increases at lower pH and during Phase 3, giving a sharp "pool" odor.
  • DPD reading: free chlorine read within 1 minute is accurate. Waiting longer lets monochloramine react, so free reads falsely high. Each reading has ±0.03 mg/L random noise.
  • Simplified for teaching: real curves depend on many more factors. Always confirm with a bench test on your own water.
